Join the Team

No prior rowing experience is required. Whether you're an experienced oarsman, coxswain, or brand-new athlete looking to compete at a collegiate level, we'll teach you the fundamentals and get you on the water.

On The Water

Where We Row

We row out of Bladensburg Waterfront Park, located on the Anacostia River in Bladensburg, Maryland. The park is around a 15-minute drive from campus.

Winter & Land Training

Indoor Training Facility

When we can't go out on the water, we train at the team's Erg Room in Cole Field House. Training on the erg allows us to put in work off the water and stay in shape during the winter months.

Competition

Regattas & Travel

Each season, the team competes at several regattas. They are perfect opportunities for us to show off our hard work and represent the University.

Team Culture

Social

When we are not training or competing, we spend plenty of time together as a team. In addition to hanging out around campus, we host social events with other clubs, and a formal at the end of the year.

Why Row at Maryland?

Maryland Men's Crew is a competitive collegiate club rowing program at the University of Maryland. Most of our athletes had zero rowing experience prior to college—if you are competitive, coachable, and committed to hard work, you can earn a seat in a racing shell.

Novice Program

Dedicated coaching to teach technique, boat handling, and racing.

Coxswains Needed

Steer the boat, lead the crew, and call race strategy on the water.

Typical Practice Schedule

  • Weekday Water Sessions Early Morning @ Bladensburg Waterfront Park
  • Indoor Erg Sessions Morning & Afternoon @ Cole Field House Erg Room
  • Weekend Sessions Morning @ Bladensburg Waterfront Park

* Morning sessions wrap up ahead of daytime campus classes.

On-water practices take place out of Bladensburg Waterfront Park on the Anacostia River (about a 15-minute drive from campus). When weather or winter conditions keep us off the water, we train indoors at the team's Erg Room in Cole Field House.
Practices are scheduled around students' academic schedules so athletes can balance coursework, labs, and campus commitments while competing at regattas.
